Divine Mercy

Encounter the Father's mercy revealed in Jesus Christ and learn how Catholic Divine Mercy devotion belongs within the Gospel and sacramental life.

Divine Mercy begins with who God is: the Father rich in mercy, revealed to us in Jesus Christ. Catholic devotion to Divine Mercy should therefore lead to deeper trust in Christ, conversion, sacramental life, forgiveness and concrete works of mercy.

👁️

Trust

Christian trust rests on Christ's saving love, not on a magical formula or private revelation detached from the Gospel.

🕯️

Conversion

Mercy does not deny sin; it calls us out of sin and toward reconciliation with God and neighbor.

🤲

Merciful action

Receiving mercy should make Christians more ready to forgive, serve the vulnerable and become signs of the Father's compassion.

St. John Paul II's encyclical Dives in Misericordia presents Christ as the one who makes the Father's mercy present and visible in history.

Divine Mercy devotion and St. Faustina

St. Maria Faustina Kowalska (1905–1938), a Polish religious sister, is associated with the modern Divine Mercy devotion. The devotion includes the image of the Merciful Jesus, the Chaplet of Divine Mercy, the Hour of Great Mercy, Divine Mercy Sunday and a spirituality of trust and mercy.

Catholic discernment: private revelations do not add to the deposit of faith. Their role is to help believers live the Gospel more fully in a particular period of history. Scripture, Tradition and the Church's Magisterium remain normative.

A simple daily pattern

  1. Morning trust. Offer the day to Jesus and ask for the grace to trust His mercy.
  2. Scripture. Read a Gospel passage and listen for Christ's call to conversion and mercy.
  3. Three o'clock remembrance. When possible, pause briefly to remember the Passion of Christ.
  4. Chaplet or another approved prayer. Intercede for sinners, the suffering, the dying and the whole world.
  5. Work of mercy. Perform one concrete spiritual or corporal work of mercy.
