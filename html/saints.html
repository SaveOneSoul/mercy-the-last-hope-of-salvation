Saints of the Church

Discover Catholic saints as witnesses of grace, models of discipleship and members of the communion of saints.

The Church venerates the saints because God's grace shines in their lives. Catholic veneration is not adoration: worship belongs to God alone. The saints are honored as members of Christ and examples of holiness, and Christians ask their intercession within the communion of saints.

St. Faustina Kowalska

Apostle of Divine Mercy; religious sister associated with the modern Divine Mercy devotion.

St. John Paul II

Pope, evangelist of Divine Mercy, author of Dives in Misericordia.

St. Carlo Acutis

Young lay Catholic known for Eucharistic devotion and digital evangelization; canonized in 2025.

St. Thérèse of Lisieux

Doctor of the Church; witness to confidence in God's merciful love through the 'little way'.

St. Francis of Assisi

Witness to evangelical poverty, peace, fraternity and love of creation.

St. Teresa of Calcutta

Missionary of Charity whose service focused on Christ in the poorest and most abandoned.

St. Augustine

Bishop and Doctor of the Church; major theologian of grace, conversion and the Christian life.

St. Teresa of Ávila

Carmelite reformer and Doctor of the Church; major teacher of contemplative prayer.

St. John of the Cross

Carmelite mystic and Doctor of the Church; teacher of purification and union with God.

How canonization works

Causes of saints involve a canonical process under ecclesiastical authority. The Dicastery for the Causes of Saints oversees norms and examines causes before matters are presented to the Roman Pontiff.

Editorial rule for future expansion: each saint profile should include feast day, state of life, century, short biography, spiritual themes, official Church source where available, and clear distinction between historically documented facts and pious legends.
