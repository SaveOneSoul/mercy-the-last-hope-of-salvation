Holy Spirit & Baptism in the Holy Spirit

Catholic teaching on the Holy Spirit, Baptism, Confirmation, gifts, fruits, charisms and the Charismatic expression 'Baptism in the Holy Spirit'.

The Holy Spirit is the third Person of the Blessed Trinity, Lord and giver of life. Christian life in the Spirit begins sacramentally in Baptism, is strengthened in Confirmation and is continually nourished in the life of grace, prayer, the Word and the Eucharist.

Baptism

Baptism is the sacrament of new birth in Christ. Together with Confirmation and the Eucharist it belongs to Christian initiation.

Confirmation

The Catechism describes Confirmation as bringing a fuller outpouring of the Holy Spirit and strengthening the baptized for Christian witness.

What is “Baptism in the Holy Spirit”?

In the Catholic Charismatic Renewal, this expression normally refers to a powerful experiential renewal or “coming alive” of baptismal and confirmation graces, often accompanied by deeper conversion, prayer, praise, love of Scripture, desire for the sacraments and openness to charisms for service.

Doctrinal safeguard: Baptism in the Holy Spirit is not an eighth sacrament, a replacement for sacramental Baptism, or a claim that validly baptized Catholics previously lacked the Holy Spirit. CHARIS explicitly describes it as an experience that brings to life the graces of Baptism and Confirmation.

Discernment of charisms

Charisms are gifts for building up the Church and serving others. They require humility, discernment, ecclesial communion and moral integrity; extraordinary experiences are never a license to bypass Church authority or sound pastoral judgment.
