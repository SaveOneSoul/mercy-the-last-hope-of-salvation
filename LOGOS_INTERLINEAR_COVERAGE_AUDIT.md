# Logos 73-Book Interlinear Coverage Audit

The canonical coverage matrix is generated by `tools/audit_logos_73_book_interlinear.py` from the installed corpus files and is published by the Logos corpus workflow as:

- `LOGOS_INTERLINEAR_COVERAGE.md` — human-readable 73-book matrix.
- `cloud-backend/app/logos_interlinear/coverage-audit.json` — machine-readable audit evidence.

## What the audit verifies

For every book in the Catholic 73-book canon, the audit records and validates:

- Douay-Rheims 1899 coverage;
- Hebrew/Aramaic OSHB/WLC coverage where a Masoretic witness exists;
- Septuagint or New Testament Greek surface coverage;
- Greek lemma/POS/morphology availability and known gaps;
- Clementine Vulgate coverage;
- source-to-Douay chapter/verse identity or explicit mapping status;
- source provenance and licence;
- known source, versification, and linguistic-annotation limitations.

The audit opens actual per-book corpus files rather than trusting headline manifest counts alone. It fails if a required canonical book, production source, licence gate, or expected corpus partition disappears. Textual and versification differences are reported as evidence; they are never silently normalized or fabricated away.

## CI behavior

On pull requests, the corpus workflow rebuilds the production corpora in temporary directories, runs the audit against those generated packages, and uploads the JSON and Markdown matrix as the `logos-73-book-interlinear-coverage` workflow artifact.

On `main`, the workflow vendors the production corpora, regenerates the audit deterministically, and commits the two canonical audit outputs whenever the evidence changes.
