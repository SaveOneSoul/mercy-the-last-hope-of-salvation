Catholic Theology · Terms & Concepts

Theological Glossary

Concise definitions for recurring theological vocabulary, with links back to the disciplines where each term belongs.

homoousios

Greek for “of the same substance/essence.” Used at Nicaea to confess that the Son is consubstantial with the Father.

Trinitarian Theology

hypostasis

In mature Trinitarian usage, a concrete subsistence or Person. The term’s meaning developed during fourth-century debates.

Trinitarian Theology

ousia

Greek for essence or substance; in Trinitarian theology, the one divine essence shared wholly by Father, Son and Holy Spirit.

Trinitarian Theology

theosis

Participation by grace in the divine life. It never means becoming God by nature or ceasing to be a creature.

Spiritual Theology

transubstantiation

The change of the whole substance of bread and wine into the Body and Blood of Christ, while the appearances remain.

Sacramental Theology

epiclesis

The liturgical invocation of the Holy Spirit, especially in the Eucharistic Prayer.

Liturgical Theology

anamnesis

Liturgical memorial that sacramentally proclaims and makes present the saving mystery of Christ, especially in the Eucharist.

Liturgical Theology

justification

God’s gracious action forgiving sins and making the person righteous, incorporating the believer into life in Christ.

Soteriology / Moral Theology

sanctification

Growth in holiness through grace, charity, virtue, sacramental life and cooperation with the Holy Spirit.

Moral / Spiritual Theology

charism

A grace of the Holy Spirit given for the building up of the Church and service of the common good.

Pneumatology / CHARIS

kerygma

The foundational proclamation of Jesus Christ—His death, Resurrection, lordship, saving grace and call to conversion and faith.

Evangelization

mystagogy

Post-initiation instruction that leads believers more deeply into the meaning of the sacraments and liturgy.

Sacramental Theology

soteriology

Theological study of salvation: Christ’s saving work, grace, redemption, justification, sanctification and participation in divine life.

Christology

eschatology

Theological study of the last things: death, judgment, heaven, purgatory, hell, resurrection, Christ’s return and new creation.

Eschatology

lex orandi

“The law of prayer.” A shorthand for the relationship between the Church’s worship and the faith she confesses.

Liturgy / Tradition

economy of salvation

God’s historical plan and action by which creation is brought to fulfillment through Christ in the Holy Spirit.

Fundamental Theology

recapitulation

Patristic theme, especially in Irenaeus, describing Christ as gathering up and restoring human history in Himself.

Christology / Patrology

perichoresis

The mutual indwelling of the divine Persons without confusion or division.

Trinitarian Theology

ex opere operato

Sacramental principle that a sacrament’s efficacy comes from Christ’s action, not from the personal holiness of the minister.

Sacramental Theology

sensus fidei

The Spirit-assisted capacity of the faithful, in communion with the Church, to recognize and adhere to the truth of faith.
