Source-rights and versification gate

The Douay-Rheims 1899 remains the primary Catholic reading text. Hebrew/Aramaic uses the pinned OSHB/WLC production package. The protocanonical Greek OT uses pinned First1K/Swete witnesses where available; Ecclesiastes is transparently sourced from a separately pinned Greek Wikisource ecclesiastical LXX transcription because the pinned First1K tree contains only its CTS metadata stub and no Greek text blob. Catholic deuterocanonical/additions Greek witnesses remain in the accepted Swete package. All Greek source data stays in a CC BY-SA 4.0 ShareAlike partition. Latin uses the pinned public-domain Clementine Vulgate transcription. Logos adds semantic glosses only from pinned licensed sources, excludes restricted Hebrew definition material, never invents contextual meaning, and does not silently force source verses into Douay numbering when a validated mapping is absent.

Catholic biblical method

Literal sense, source languages, Tradition and history kept distinct

Complete Catholic English canon

The public-domain Douay-Rheims 1899 corpus provides all 73 Catholic books locally from a pinned, integrity-verified source and remains the primary Bible layer.

Original-language Old Testament workspace

Old Testament study can combine OSHB/WLC Hebrew-Aramaic, Septuagint Greek, the Clementine Latin Vulgate and Douay-Rheims English. Greek provenance is book-specific: First1K/Swete is used where its pinned text exists, while Ecclesiastes is identified separately as the pinned Greek Wikisource ecclesiastical LXX witness. The Greek ShareAlike corpus stays isolated, and verse alignment is shown only when verified rather than guessed.

History, archives & archaeology

Secular sources, royal annals, inscriptions, coins and archaeology are classified as direct attestation, synchronism, contextual evidence, disputed identification or reconstruction. They are never treated as automatic proof of theology.

Commentary traditions

Catholic, Jewish, Eastern Christian, Protestant/Evangelical and ecumenical scholarship can be compared while Catholic Magisterial teaching remains clearly identified and distinct.
