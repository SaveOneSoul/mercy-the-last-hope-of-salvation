Save One Soul Campaign

The mission

“Save One Soul” is an evangelization campaign built around intercessory prayer, sacrifice, works of mercy, patient accompaniment and bringing people toward a living relationship with Jesus Christ and His Church. No human being can mechanically guarantee another person’s salvation. Salvation is God’s grace, freely offered and freely received; the Christian cooperates through prayer, witness, sacrifice and love.

1. Pray

Intercede daily for one person by name, especially for conversion, healing, reconciliation and final perseverance.

2. Offer

Unite ordinary sacrifices, fasting, suffering and works of mercy to Christ’s Passion for the spiritual good of souls.

3. Accompany

Listen without manipulation; witness to Christ; invite the person to prayer, Scripture, Confession, Holy Mass and Christian community.

St. Faustina and prayer for the conversion of sinners

In Diary §1397, St. Faustina records Jesus teaching that prayer for the conversion of sinners is especially pleasing to Him and consoles Him. In §§186–187, she records a specific invitation to call upon Divine Mercy for sinners and links the following prayer with the grace of conversion when it is offered with contrition and faith.

Elsewhere in the Diary, Faustina repeatedly understands her apostolic mission as winning souls for Christ through prayer, sacrifice, trust and proclamation of mercy. The Church receives these passages as private revelation: they can encourage Christian life, but they do not add to the deposit of faith and must always be interpreted in harmony with Scripture, Tradition and the Magisterium.

Prayer: O Blood and Water

“O Blood and Water, which gushed forth from the Heart of Jesus as a fount of Mercy for us, I trust in You.”

Diary §§186–187

The prayer directs trust to Jesus through the mystery signified by the Blood and Water flowing from His pierced side (cf. John 19:34). In Catholic spirituality these signs naturally call to mind Christ’s saving Passion and the sacramental life flowing from Him.

The 33 Acts of Trust — Diary §341

What the Diary index records: the “O Blood and Water” prayer is associated in Diary §341 with 33 repetitions for the intention of the Holy Father. This is distinct from the later Divine Mercy Chaplet and should not be confused with it.

How to pray the 33 acts

Choose the intention — traditionally, in this Diary context, the Holy Father’s intention — and recollect yourself before Christ. Then slowly pray:

O Blood and Water, which gushed forth from the Heart of Jesus as a fount of Mercy for us, I trust in You.

Repeat the prayer 33 times.

The power of prayer does not lie in a magical number. The repetitions are acts of persevering faith and trust. For conversion intentions, pray with contrition, charity and confidence in God’s mercy, while leaving the person and the manner of God’s action to divine providence.

Pro-Life — Catholic teaching on abortion and human dignity

Every human life has God-given dignity. Catholic pro-life teaching begins with the dignity of every person, not with political party identity. The unborn child, the mother, the father, the poor, the disabled, the sick and the elderly all deserve truth, protection, compassionate care and concrete solidarity.

Life from conception

The Catechism teaches that human life must be respected and protected from conception. The unborn child must be treated with the respect owed to human life and protected in law and in society. The Church therefore teaches that direct abortion — abortion intended as an end or as a means — is gravely contrary to the moral law.

Why the Church teaches this

Catholic teaching rests on the sacredness of human life and the equal dignity of the innocent. Human worth does not depend on age, health, wantedness, ability, economic status or stage of development. St. John Paul II’s Evangelium Vitae places the protection of unborn life within the wider “Gospel of Life,” calling society to receive every human person as a gift rather than an object of use or disposal.

Mercy, healing and accompaniment

The Church’s condemnation of abortion is never permission to condemn or abandon a woman who has undergone one, nor a father, family member or other person involved. The Catechism itself explains that the Church’s canonical seriousness does not restrict God’s mercy. Evangelium Vitae speaks pastorally to women who have had abortions, inviting them not to surrender to discouragement but to seek repentance, reconciliation, forgiveness and peace in Christ.

A genuinely Catholic pro-life ministry therefore includes practical accompaniment: helping mothers in difficult pregnancies, supporting fathers and families, encouraging responsible medical and social care, material assistance, safe housing where possible, adoption and foster-care support, and compassionate post-abortion healing.

A simple 7-day Save One Soul practice

  1. Choose one person and keep the intention confidential.
  2. Pray the “O Blood and Water” prayer for the person’s conversion.
  3. Offer one decade of the Rosary each day.
  4. Pray the Divine Mercy Chaplet during the week.
  5. Offer one concrete act of self-denial, service or mercy.
  6. Ask the Holy Spirit for wisdom before speaking or acting.
  7. Entrust the person completely to Jesus, without pressure, manipulation or anxiety.
