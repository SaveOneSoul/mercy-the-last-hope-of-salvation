Counselling courses

Learn to listen with care, respect boundaries and connect people with appropriate support. Choose a foundations course or a pastoral accompaniment course.

Each course has 10 units and 20 internal formation credits. Read the sources, answer the knowledge checks and complete the fictional case exercises. These are introductory, non-accredited courses. Completion does not qualify or license anyone to practise psychology, counselling or psychotherapy.

Counselling Foundations

For introductory learning and volunteer awareness. Psychology Foundations is helpful preparation. This is not supervised clinical training.

  1. Helping roles and professional boundariesDistinguish supportive listening, psychotherapy and spiritual direction.
  2. Empathy and attentive listeningReflect meaning while checking your understanding.
  3. Questions, silence and summariesUse questions to support understanding rather than interrogation.
  4. Consent, privacy and confidentialityExplain privacy limits before inviting disclosure.
  5. Collaborative goals and strengthsAgree one manageable step chosen by the person.
  6. Stress support and practical copingOffer low-risk practices as choices.
  7. Grief and lossSupport the person’s own experience without imposing a timeline.
  8. Safety, safeguarding and urgent helpPrioritise immediate safety and qualified assistance.
  9. Referral, collaboration and supervisionMake a referral supportive and practical.
  10. A bounded helping conversationIntegrate listening, consent, goals and referral in a fictional case.

Pastoral Accompaniment

Build on the foundations course to explore optional faith support, bereavement, illness, safeguarding and referral. Pastoral care and qualified clinical care can work alongside one another.

  1. Pastoral presence and role clarityExplain the distinction between spiritual accompaniment and clinical care.
  2. Spiritual listening without pressureListen to faith questions without forcing a conclusion.
  3. Conscience, guilt and persistent distressAvoid diagnosing or reinforcing cycles of reassurance.
  4. Illness, disability and healing prayerOffer prayer alongside practical and professional care.
  5. Bereavement and lamentMake room for grief without requiring an explanation.
  6. Family conflict, abuse and safeguardingDistinguish ordinary disagreement from danger requiring specialist help.
  7. Discernment and unusual experiencesRespect spiritual meaning without diagnosing supernatural causes.
  8. Cultural humility and inclusive welcomeAsk about the person’s preferences rather than making group assumptions.
  9. Helper wellbeing, supervision and limitsPlan sustainable service with clear boundaries.
  10. An integrated pastoral care planCombine chosen spiritual support with practical help and referral.
