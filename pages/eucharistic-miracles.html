Eucharistic Miracles

A careful Catholic introduction to reported Eucharistic miracles and the Real Presence.

Catholic caution: faith in the Real Presence rests on Christ’s words, Apostolic faith and the Church’s sacramental doctrine—not on extraordinary phenomena. Reports of Eucharistic miracles have different levels of historical evidence and ecclesial recognition.

What Catholics believe

At Mass, by the consecration, the bread and wine become the Body and Blood of Christ while the appearances of bread and wine remain. Eucharistic miracles are devotional signs and do not replace the Eucharist itself.

Lanciano, Italy

Traditionally dated to the 8th century

One of the best-known ancient Eucharistic miracle traditions; preserved at the Church of San Francesco.

Bolsena–Orvieto, Italy

1263

Tradition connects a bleeding corporal at Bolsena with Pope Urban IV and the wider history surrounding Corpus Christi.

Santarem, Portugal

13th century

A long-standing Portuguese Eucharistic miracle tradition centered on a consecrated Host preserved in Santarém.

Siena, Italy

1730

Consecrated Hosts stolen and later recovered are preserved and periodically examined as part of a long local devotional tradition.

Tixtla, Mexico

2006

A reported Eucharistic event investigated at diocesan level; claims should be read with the competent Church authority’s documentation.

Sokółka, Poland

2008

A reported event examined by the Archdiocese of Białystok and presented locally as a Eucharistic sign.

Legnica, Poland

2013

A reported event investigated by the Diocese of Legnica and authorized for local veneration with pastoral guidance.

Carlo Acutis Exhibition

International catalogue

St. Carlo Acutis helped create an international exhibition cataloguing reported Eucharistic miracles from many countries.

St. Carlo Acutis and the international exhibition

St. Carlo Acutis was deeply devoted to the Eucharist and helped realize an international exhibition on Eucharistic miracles. His official association site describes the catalogue as a Vatican International Exhibition and provides information about obtaining the catalogue.
