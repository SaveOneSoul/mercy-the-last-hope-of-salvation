The Eucharist: Presence, Sacrifice, Communion & Signs

Begin with what the Catholic Church believes about the Eucharist. Then explore historic Eucharistic-miracle traditions, shrine records and diocesan investigations without confusing extraordinary signs with the foundation of faith.

Catholic principle. Faith in the Eucharist does not depend on any reported miracle or laboratory finding. Catholics believe in the Real Presence because of Christ’s words, Apostolic faith and the Church’s sacramental teaching. Extraordinary signs may encourage devotion, but they do not replace doctrine.
What Catholics believe

The mystery before the miracles

The Eucharist is the sacramental making-present of Christ’s Paschal sacrifice and the gift of Christ Himself—Body, Blood, Soul and Divinity—under the appearances of bread and wine.

Presence

Christ is truly present

The Eucharistic presence is real, true and substantial. The consecrated species are not merely reminders or symbols detached from the reality they signify.

Sacrifice

The one sacrifice made present

The Mass does not repeat Calvary. The one sacrifice of Christ is sacramentally made present and offered in the Church’s worship.

Communion

Union with Christ and His Body

Holy Communion deepens union with Christ, strengthens ecclesial communion, forgives venial sins and commits the faithful to charity.

Adoration

Worship beyond Mass

Because Christ remains present under the consecrated species, the Church reserves and adores the Blessed Sacrament outside Mass.

Transubstantiation

The Church uses this term for the conversion of the whole substance of bread into the substance of Christ’s Body and the whole substance of wine into the substance of His Blood, while the sensible appearances remain.

Read the Catechism on the Eucharist

Not a scientific mechanism

“Substance” in this doctrine is metaphysical, not a claim that consecration can be reduced to ordinary physical or chemical change. Catholic Eucharistic theology therefore cannot be proved or disproved by a laboratory test alone.

Biblical foundation

Read the Eucharist in Scripture

The Church reads the Eucharistic mystery across the whole biblical economy: Passover, manna, sacrifice, covenant, the Bread of Life discourse, the Last Supper and the apostolic celebration of the Lord’s Supper.

Promise

John 6

Jesus identifies Himself as the Bread of Life and speaks of giving His flesh for the life of the world.

Institution

Last Supper

Matthew 26:26–28, Mark 14:22–24 and Luke 22:19–20 preserve the words over bread and wine and the command to do this in remembrance of Christ.

Apostolic Church

1 Corinthians 10–11

St. Paul speaks of participation in the Body and Blood of Christ and warns against receiving the Eucharist unworthily.

Evidence discipline

How to read Eucharistic-miracle claims

Different cases have different kinds of evidence and ecclesial status. This page does not flatten shrine tradition, historical documentation, diocesan investigation and scientific testimony into one category.

1. Identify the Church source

Prefer shrine, parish, cathedral, diocesan or Holy See records over anonymous summaries and viral claims.

2. Separate claim from doctrine

A bishop’s permission for devotion, a commission report and a scientific opinion are not the same thing as a dogmatic definition.

3. Preserve uncertainty

Where the evidence is historical tradition, say so. Where a diocese investigated a modern event, identify that level of authority without upgrading it into universal Church teaching.

Historic shrine tradition

Lanciano, Italy

The Franciscan sanctuary preserves the long-standing Lanciano Eucharistic miracle tradition and documents twentieth-century scientific examinations commissioned around the relics.

Cathedral record

Bolsena–Orvieto, Italy

Orvieto Cathedral preserves the Sacred Corporal associated with the 1263 Bolsena tradition in the Chapel of the Corporal. The cathedral explicitly connects this tradition with the history of Corpus Christi devotion.

Long-standing devotion

Santarém, Portugal

The Sanctuary of the Most Holy Miracle preserves a centuries-old Portuguese Eucharistic tradition that continues to be studied, published and celebrated locally.

Siena, Italy

The Basilica of San Francesco is associated with the tradition of consecrated Hosts stolen in 1730, recovered and preserved through subsequent centuries.

Archdiocesan investigation

Sokółka, Poland

After a 2008 event, an ecclesiastical commission established by the Archbishop of Białystok examined witnesses and pathology reports. The archdiocesan communication presents the event as consonant with Eucharistic faith while preserving the Church’s doctrinal framework.

Diocesan case

Legnica, Poland

The Legnica case is a modern reported Eucharistic event handled at diocesan level and associated with St. Hyacinth Church. It should be presented as a local ecclesial case rather than as a universal doctrinal proof.

Worldwide catalogue

St. Carlo Acutis & the international exhibition

St. Carlo Acutis helped realize an international catalogue and exhibition of Eucharistic-miracle traditions from many countries. It is useful as a devotional discovery tool, while individual cases should still be checked against their own competent Church sources.

Use the catalogue well

St. Thomas Aquinas

Substance, presence and sacramental fruit

Aquinas gives the classical theological architecture for understanding Eucharistic conversion and presence without confusing sacramental reality with ordinary material change.

Summa III, q.75

The conversion

St. Thomas asks how the substance of bread and wine is changed into the Body and Blood of Christ.

Read q.75

Summa III, q.76

The whole Christ

Christ is truly present under the sacramental species; Eucharistic presence is not a crude spatial containment.

Read q.76

Summa III, q.79

The effects

The sacrament nourishes spiritual life, strengthens charity and unites the communicant more deeply to Christ.

Read q.79

Ancient witness

The Church Fathers

Early Christian writers witness to a Eucharistic faith that is sacramental, ecclesial and strongly realist.

St. Ignatius of Antioch

His Letter to the Smyrnaeans links orthodox Christian confession with recognition of the Eucharist as the flesh of Jesus Christ.

Read Smyrnaeans

St. Justin Martyr

Justin’s First Apology describes the Eucharistic assembly and insists that Christians do not receive the Eucharistic food as ordinary bread and drink.

Read First Apology 65–67

St. Cyril of Jerusalem

His mystagogical catecheses teach newly baptized Christians to approach the Eucharistic mystery with faith rather than judging only by the senses.

Modern Magisterium

Papal Eucharistic teaching

Recent popes repeatedly connect Eucharistic doctrine with reverent worship, ecclesial communion, mission and the transformation of Christian life.

1965

St. Paul VI — Mysterium Fidei

A major defense and clarification of Catholic Eucharistic faith and the language of transubstantiation.

Read Mysterium Fidei

2003

St. John Paul II — Ecclesia de Eucharistia

The Eucharist builds the Church and stands at the center of ecclesial life and mission.

Read Ecclesia de Eucharistia

2007

Benedict XVI — Sacramentum Caritatis

Worship, celebration and Christian life belong together in the Eucharistic mystery.

Read Sacramentum Caritatis

2022

Liturgical formation should recover wonder before the Paschal mystery while avoiding both ritualism and subjectivism.
