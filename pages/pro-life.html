Pro-Life

Every human life has God-given dignity. Catholic pro-life teaching is broader than party politics. It begins with the conviction that every human person is created in the image of God and must be treated with truth, protection, love and practical solidarity from conception to natural death.

The foundation: the dignity of the human person

The Church’s defense of unborn life begins with the dignity of the human person. Human worth is not granted by the state, health, age, ability, economic usefulness, wantedness or another person’s choice. It belongs to every human being because human life is created by God and called to communion with Him.

The Catechism teaches that human life must be respected and protected absolutely from the moment of conception and that, from the first moment of existence, a human being must be recognized as having the rights of a person, above all the inviolable right to life.

Embryonic life is not a mere collection of cells. The Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith teaches in Dignitas Personae that the human embryonic body develops progressively according to a well-defined program and that the human being must be respected and treated as a person from conception. The Pontifical Academy for Life likewise described embryonic development as continuous, coordinated and gradual from fertilization.

Therefore the unborn child, the mother, the father, persons with disabilities, the sick, the poor, the elderly and every vulnerable person must be treated with dignity. A Catholic culture of life rejects both violence against innocent life and indifference toward people in difficult circumstances.

Catholic teaching on abortion

Since the first century, the Church has consistently rejected procured abortion. The Catechism teaches that direct abortion — abortion willed either as an end or as a means — is gravely contrary to the moral law.

This moral judgment does not change according to whether a child is healthy or disabled, planned or unplanned, poor or rich, male or female. The dignity of the child does not depend on circumstances.

St. John Paul II’s Evangelium Vitae situates this teaching within the wider Christian “Gospel of Life”: human life is a gift to be welcomed, protected and served. The encyclical also recalls the Church’s witness from the earliest Christian centuries against abortion and infanticide.

The Second Vatican Council teaches in Gaudium et Spes §51 that life must be guarded with the greatest care from conception and strongly condemns abortion and infanticide.

Important moral and medical distinctions

Miscarriage / spontaneous abortion

In medical terminology, “spontaneous abortion” means miscarriage: a pregnancy loss that is not deliberately chosen. It is morally different from procured abortion because the death of the unborn child is not willed.

Direct abortion

A direct abortion intentionally targets the death of the unborn child either as the goal itself or as the chosen means to another goal. Catholic teaching holds that such an act is intrinsically wrong.

Treatment of serious maternal pathology

Catholic moral theology distinguishes direct abortion from proportionate medical treatment directed at a serious pathology in the mother when the child’s death is neither intended as the goal nor chosen as the means. Difficult cases require competent physicians and sound Catholic bioethical guidance.

Both mother and child deserve care. Catholic teaching does not deny the seriousness of maternal illness, hemorrhage, cancer, ectopic pregnancy or other emergencies. It asks clinicians to distinguish the intended therapeutic act from the foreseen but unintended loss of the child and to respect both patients as far as medically possible.

Canon Law, abortion and sacramental forgiveness

Canon 1397 §2: the current Code of Canon Law provides a latae sententiae excommunication for a person who actually procures a completed abortion, subject to the general rules of penal law.

This does not mean every person connected with an abortion automatically incurs the penalty in every circumstance. Canonical penalties depend on imputability and the conditions established by Church law. Canons 1321–1324 address age, lack of reason, ignorance, grave fear, necessity and other factors that can remove or diminish imputability. Individual cases should be handled pastorally and canonically, not judged casually by others.

The canonical penalty is distinct from sacramental forgiveness. In Misericordia et Misera §12, Pope Francis granted all priests, by virtue of their ministry, the faculty to absolve those who have committed the sin of procured abortion. He simultaneously reaffirmed its gravity and stressed that no repentant sinner lies beyond God’s mercy.

The Gospel of Life

Evangelium Vitae calls Catholics not merely to oppose abortion but to build a culture in which human life is welcomed, protected and served. That means confronting fear, poverty, abandonment, coercion, abuse, housing insecurity, medical needs and other pressures that can make pregnancy seem impossible.

40 Days for Life

Prayerful and peaceful pro-life witness. 40 Days for Life is an independent international pro-life campaign. It is not an office or agency of the Holy See, but its stated approach is strongly centered on prayer, fasting and peaceful public witness for an end to abortion.

Prayer & fasting

Participants unite in focused prayer and fasting, asking God for conversion of hearts, protection of unborn children and compassionate support for mothers, fathers and families.

Peaceful vigil

The campaign organizes peaceful vigils near abortion facilities where legally permitted. Catholic participation should always remain nonviolent, respectful, lawful and free from harassment.

Community outreach

The campaign encourages local communities to educate, pray, accompany families and connect people in crisis pregnancies with life-affirming support.

Does abortion “cry to heaven for justice”?

The Catechism’s traditional list of “sins that cry to heaven” (CCC 1867) includes the blood of Abel, the sin of the Sodomites, the cry of the oppressed people in Egypt, the cry of the foreigner, widow and orphan, and injustice to the wage earner.

The Catechism does not explicitly name abortion in CCC 1867. It would therefore be inaccurate to claim that it formally lists abortion there. Abortion is instead separately taught to be gravely contrary to the moral law. The biblical image of Abel’s innocent blood crying from the ground can illuminate the gravity of innocent bloodshed without misquoting the Catechism.

The older expression “crying to heaven for vengeance” means a cry to God for justice, never permission for personal revenge or violence. Christian pro-life witness must remain peaceful, truthful and merciful.

Mercy, repentance and healing after abortion

No one is beyond God’s mercy. The Church’s condemnation of abortion is never permission to despise, shame or abandon a woman who has undergone one, a father who participated, or anyone else involved.

Those who carry guilt, grief or trauma after abortion should be invited toward Christ, not driven away from Him. The Church offers repentance, Sacramental Confession, spiritual accompaniment and the hope of reconciliation. In Evangelium Vitae §99, St. John Paul II addresses women who have had abortions with both moral truth and hope, inviting them to entrust themselves to the Father’s mercy and peace.

What a Catholic pro-life ministry should do

Support mothers

Offer practical help during difficult pregnancies: medical referrals, food, transportation, clothing, housing assistance where available, emotional support and accompaniment.

Support fathers & families

Encourage responsibility, reconciliation, material support, fatherhood and stable environments for children.

Protect the vulnerable

Care for children with disabilities, mothers in crisis, victims of coercion, the poor, the sick and those whose lives are treated as burdens.

Offer post-abortion healing

Respond without contempt. Encourage Confession, spiritual direction, counseling where appropriate and confidence in Christ’s mercy.

Pray and witness peacefully

Pray for mothers, fathers, children, medical workers, lawmakers and the conversion of hearts. Christian witness must reject harassment and violence.

Build a culture of life

Teach chastity, faithful marriage, responsible parenthood, adoption, care for persons with disabilities and practical solidarity with families in need.

A prayer for life

Lord Jesus Christ, Author of life and Fountain of mercy, protect every child in the womb, strengthen every mother in fear or difficulty, give courage and responsibility to fathers, wisdom to families and medical workers, and conversion of heart to all who are tempted to treat human life as disposable. Heal those wounded by abortion, lead them to Your mercy, and make Your Church a place of truth, protection, compassion and hope. Amen.
