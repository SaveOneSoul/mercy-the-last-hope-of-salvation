Patristic Primary Text Library

Use the Fathers study notes together with the primary texts. This library points to complete public-domain translations where available, surviving fragments where a work is lost, and older scholarly editions for textual research.

Important distinction: not every work survives complete, and not every modern translation may legally be republished. This library therefore separates complete public-domain texts, surviving fragments, and modern copyrighted editions. We should not label a lost or fragmentary work as a complete text.
Complete ancient text available

The Didache

The Didache is an anonymous early Church manual rather than the writing of a named Father. A complete English text is available online through New Advent. It covers the Two Ways, Baptism, fasting, the Lord’s Prayer, Eucharistic thanksgiving, itinerant ministers, bishops and deacons, and eschatological vigilance.

Origen: Scripture, Homilies, Contra Celsum and the Hexapla

For Origen, the site should distinguish his surviving works from lost works and later reconstructions. Against Celsus and On First Principles are available in older public-domain English translations. Many homilies and commentaries survive wholly or partially, often through Rufinus or Jerome. The Hexapla itself does not survive as a complete six-column manuscript; what survives are fragments, quotations, marginal readings and later scholarly reconstructions.

The Hexapla

Because Origen’s original Hexapla is lost, no website can honestly provide “the full original Hexapla.” For serious study, the classic public-domain reconstruction is Frederick Field’s 1875 Origenis Hexaplorum quae supersunt (“what survives of the Hexapla”), in two volumes. It gathers surviving fragments from ancient witnesses.

Field: surviving Hexapla fragmentsInternet Archive: Field vol. 1

Recommended Origen study order

  1. Begin with selected homilies and biblical commentaries.
  2. Read Against Celsus for mature apologetic theology.
  3. Approach On First Principles with historical and doctrinal caution.
  4. Use Field’s Hexapla fragments for textual criticism and comparison of ancient Greek versions.
  5. Compare speculative claims with later conciliar and magisterial doctrine rather than treating every Origenian proposal as Catholic teaching.

Large public-domain collections

The most practical way to give students access to the Fathers’ full surviving works is to use the public-domain Ante-Nicene Fathers and Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers collections. These contain entire works, homilies, letters, treatises and fragments in older English translations.
