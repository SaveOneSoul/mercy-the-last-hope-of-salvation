Saints of the Church

A growing searchable directory with feast days, patronages, short original biographies and links for further reading.

Growing directory: the Roman Martyrology contains thousands of saints and blesseds. This page is a broad curated catalogue, not a claim to reproduce the whole Martyrology. Use the Vatican News Saints link below for the wider daily calendar; this directory can continue to grow release by release.

St. Joseph

Feast: March 19

Spouse of the Blessed Virgin Mary and foster father of Jesus. The Gospels portray him as a just man who obeyed God promptly and protected the Holy Family.

St. Peter the Apostle

Feast: June 29

Fisherman called by Christ to lead the Twelve; after his denial he was restored and became a principal witness to the Resurrection and shepherd of the Church at Rome.

St. Paul the Apostle

Feast: June 29

Former persecutor Saul was converted by the risen Christ and became the great missionary to the Gentiles and author of letters central to the New Testament.

St. Mary Magdalene

Feast: July 22

Disciple of Jesus who remained near the Cross and became the first recorded witness to the risen Lord in John’s Gospel.

St. Stephen

Feast: December 26

One of the first seven deacons and the Church’s first martyr, whose witness and forgiveness at death echo the Passion of Christ.

St. Ignatius of Antioch

Feast: October 17

Early bishop and martyr whose letters strongly witness to unity with the bishop, the Eucharist and fidelity to Christ.

St. Justin Martyr

Feast: June 1

Second-century philosopher and apologist who defended Christianity and left one of the earliest descriptions of the Sunday Eucharistic assembly.

St. Irenaeus of Lyons

Feast: June 28

Bishop and theologian who defended apostolic faith against Gnosticism and was proclaimed Doctor of Unity by Pope Francis.

St. Athanasius

Feast: May 2

Bishop of Alexandria and heroic defender of Christ’s full divinity during the Arian controversy.

St. Jerome

Feast: September 30

Biblical scholar and Doctor of the Church who devoted his life to Scripture and produced the great Latin translation associated with the Vulgate.

St. Augustine of Hippo

Feast: August 28

After years of searching, Augustine was converted and baptized by Ambrose, later becoming bishop of Hippo and one of the Church’s most influential theologians.

St. Monica

Feast: August 27

Mother of Augustine, remembered for persevering prayer for her son’s conversion and for patient Christian motherhood.

St. Benedict of Nursia

Feast: July 11

Father of Western monasticism whose Rule formed communities around prayer, work, obedience and stability.

St. Francis of Assisi

Feast: October 4

Merchant’s son who embraced evangelical poverty, founded the Franciscan family and renewed the Church through penance, joy and love of Christ.

St. Clare of Assisi

Feast: August 11

Follower of Francis who founded the Poor Clares and lived radical poverty, Eucharistic devotion and contemplative prayer.

St. Dominic

Feast: August 8

Founder of the Order of Preachers, joining contemplation, study and preaching in service of truth and salvation of souls.

St. Thomas Aquinas

Feast: January 28

Dominican theologian and Doctor of the Church whose synthesis of faith and reason profoundly shaped Catholic teaching, especially on the Eucharist.

St. Catherine of Siena

Feast: April 29

Dominican tertiary, mystic and Doctor of the Church who served the sick, urged reform and worked for ecclesial unity.

St. Anthony of Padua

Feast: June 13

Franciscan preacher and Doctor of the Church known for biblical learning, care for the poor and powerful preaching of conversion.

St. Ignatius of Loyola

Feast: July 31

Former soldier converted during convalescence who founded the Jesuits and developed the Spiritual Exercises for discernment and mission.

St. Francis Xavier

Feast: December 3

Jesuit missionary who preached across India and Southeast Asia and became one of the great missionary saints of the modern era.

St. Teresa of Ávila

Feast: October 15

Carmelite reformer, mystic and Doctor of the Church whose writings on prayer, especially the Interior Castle, describe growth toward transforming union with God.

St. John of the Cross

Feast: December 14

Carmelite reformer and Doctor of the Church whose poetry and theology explain purification, the dark night and transforming union.

St. Vincent de Paul

Feast: September 27

French priest who organized missions to the poor, improved clergy formation and founded communities devoted to practical charity.

St. Thérèse of Lisieux

Feast: October 1

Carmelite Doctor of the Church who taught the Little Way of spiritual childhood: trusting love expressed through ordinary acts.

St. Maximilian Kolbe

Feast: August 14

Franciscan priest and missionary who offered his life in Auschwitz in place of another prisoner.

St. Teresa Benedicta of the Cross (Edith Stein)

Feast: August 9

Jewish philosopher who became Catholic, entered Carmel and was killed at Auschwitz; her life joins reason, the Cross and contemplative faith.

St. Pio of Pietrelcina

Feast: September 23

Capuchin priest known for intense prayer, long hours hearing confessions, devotion to the Mass and care for the sick.

St. Maria Faustina Kowalska

Feast: October 5

Polish Sister of Our Lady of Mercy whose Diary records her mission to proclaim God’s mercy, trust in Jesus and prayer for sinners.

St. Teresa of Calcutta

Feast: September 5

Founder of the Missionaries of Charity who served Christ in the poorest of the poor and taught the value of small acts done with great love.

St. John Paul II

Feast: October 22

Polish pope whose long pontificate emphasized Christ, human dignity, the family, youth, Divine Mercy, the Eucharist and the new evangelization.

St. Óscar Romero

Feast: March 24

Archbishop of San Salvador and martyr who defended the dignity of the poor and victims of violence while calling all sides to conversion.

St. Carlo Acutis

Feast: October 12

Young Italian layman devoted to the Eucharist who used digital skills to catalogue Eucharistic miracles and encouraged holiness in ordinary life.

St. Lorenzo Ruiz

Feast: September 28

Filipino layman, husband and father martyred in Nagasaki after refusing to renounce Christ.

St. Andrew Kim Taegon

Feast: September 20

First Korean-born Catholic priest, martyred during persecution and remembered with the Korean Martyrs.

St. Alphonsa of the Immaculate Conception

Feast: July 28

Indian Franciscan Clarist nun who offered prolonged suffering in union with Christ and became the first woman of Indian origin canonized.

St. Kuriakose Elias Chavara

Feast: January 3

Indian Syro-Malabar priest, educator and religious founder who promoted families, schools and spiritual renewal.

St. Devasahayam Pillai

Feast: January 14

Eighteenth-century Indian lay convert and martyr whose Christian faith led to persecution and death in the Kingdom of Travancore.

St. Thomas the Apostle

Feast: July 3

Apostle remembered for moving from doubt to the confession “My Lord and my God,” and by ancient tradition connected with Christianity in India.
