Psychology

Understand behaviour, learning, emotions and relationships. Begin with evidence, practise careful thinking and respect the limits of introductory study.

Each course has 10 units and 20 internal formation credits. Read the sources, answer the knowledge checks and complete the fictional case exercises. These are introductory, non-accredited courses. Completion does not qualify or license anyone to practise psychology, counselling or psychotherapy.

Psychology Foundations

No prior psychology study is required. Begin with research methods, then explore brain and body, attention, habits, memory, emotions, development and help-seeking. All exercises can be completed with fictional examples.

  1. Psychology as a scienceDistinguish evidence, interpretation and personal opinion.
  2. Brain, body and behaviourDescribe interacting systems without assigning a vice to one brain region.
  3. Attention, consciousness and sleepDifferentiate everyday automatic processing from theoretical accounts of the unconscious.
  4. Learning and habitsExplain cues, consequences and repetition without universal promises.
  5. Memory and effective studyDistinguish encoding, storage and retrieval.
  6. Emotion, stress and regulationUse simple coping skills without suppressing or moralising feelings.
  7. Development across the lifespanExplain variation without using a stage model as a diagnosis.
  8. Personality, social influence and cultureNotice situational explanations and avoid stereotyping.
  9. Mental health literacy and help-seekingRecognise when education should lead to qualified assessment.
  10. Integrating psychology, ethics and faithKeep scientific, moral and spiritual questions distinct and in dialogue.
