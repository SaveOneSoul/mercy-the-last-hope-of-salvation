Exorcism & Deliverance

What the Catholic Church permits, what is reserved to a priest, and where to find suitable prayers.

Begin with ordinary Christian life. Prayer, the sacraments, Scripture, repentance, and pastoral guidance belong at the center of spiritual life. Distress or unusual experiences alone do not establish demonic possession.
For everyone

Personal supplication

The faithful may ask God for protection and deliverance from evil. The Church’s approved exorcism book also contains prayers in its appendix intended for use by the faithful. Use only the prayers identified for them.

Read bishops’ guidance
Reserved rite

Major exorcism

Canon 1172 requires special and express permission from the local ordinary for an exorcism over someone believed possessed. Such permission is given only to a qualified priest. A prayer group or lay person must not perform the major rite.

Read canon 1172
Discernment

Medical and pastoral care

The Catechism distinguishes illness, especially psychological illness, from demonic possession. The bishops’ guidance recommends thorough medical, psychological and psychiatric assessment before any referral to an exorcist.

Read Catechism §1673

Practical boundaries for prayer groups

Ask God for help. Pray to the Father through Christ for protection, freedom, healing and peace. Avoid claiming the authority of an appointed exorcist.

Do not use restricted formulas. The 1985 letter of the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith prohibits the faithful from using the exorcism formula against Satan and the fallen angels derived from the rite published at the order of Leo XIII.

Do not interrogate demons. The same letter warns against gatherings without the required faculty in which demons are directly questioned or their identity sought.

Seek help through the Church. If someone fears possession, contact a parish priest or diocese for pastoral discernment. Continue appropriate medical or mental health care.
