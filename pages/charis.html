Youth United for Christ — YU4C India

YU4C is the official youth ministry of CHARIS India. The national ministry grew out of earlier Catholic Charismatic youth initiatives in different regions. The former National Catholic Charismatic Youth Council (NCCYC) served youth until 2008; after consultation and discernment, a joint meeting at Nagpur on 30 November 2008 decided on the common name Youth United for Christ. YU4C was officially launched on 27 December 2008 at the KAIROS 2008 National Youth Convention in Mumbai.

CHARIS India states that YU4C is coordinated by a team drawn from different regions under guidelines approved by the CHARIS National Service of Communion (CNSC), and that the YU4C Coordinator serves ex officio in the CNSC.

National coordination

The current CHARIS India members page lists Mr. Vinit D’souza as Coordinator, YU4C, and Mr. Ajin Joseph as Youth Coordinator, Asia–Oceania. Because the public source does not publish a complete current YU4C working-team roster, this website does not invent additional names.

Theology of the Holy Spirit

The Holy Spirit is not merely a force, feeling or spiritual energy. He is the Third Divine Person of the Most Holy Trinity, true God with the Father and the Son: “the Lord, the giver of life.” The Father sends the Spirit, and Christ sends the Spirit in the economy of salvation. The Spirit reveals Christ, awakens faith, gives new birth, sanctifies the baptized, dwells in the faithful, builds up the Church and equips her with gifts for mission.

The Spirit’s work is inseparable from Christ and the Church. He brings believers into Trinitarian life, forms them as adopted children of the Father, helps them pray, produces holiness, gives gifts and charisms for the common good, and empowers the Church to proclaim Jesus as Lord. Authentic charisms are never private possessions: they are gifts for service, and their exercise requires discernment.

Scriptural anchors: John 3:5–8; John 14:16–17, 26; John 15:26; John 16:7–15; Acts 1:8; Acts 2; Romans 5:5; Romans 8:14–27; 1 Corinthians 12–14; Galatians 5:22–25.

Theology of Baptism in the Holy Spirit

Not an eighth sacrament. In Catholic Charismatic theology, Baptism in the Holy Spirit does not replace or repeat sacramental Baptism or Confirmation. The ordinary sacramental incorporation into Christ and reception of the Spirit remain foundational.

Baptism in the Holy Spirit is commonly understood as a prayerful, experiential actualization and renewal of baptismal and confirmation grace: a deeper surrender to Jesus as Lord, renewed awareness of the Father’s love, repentance, empowerment for witness, thirst for prayer and Scripture, love for the sacraments, and greater openness to the Spirit’s charisms for service.

CHARIS teaches this grace in continuity with Catholic initiation. Acts 2 links repentance, Baptism and reception of the Spirit; the Church’s sacramental life is therefore not bypassed. The experience must mature through conversion, Eucharistic life, Confession, prayer, formation, community, service and obedience to Christ.

The Duquesne Weekend

From 17–19 February 1967, students on retreat at The Ark and the Dove near Pittsburgh experienced a powerful outpouring of the Holy Spirit. The Duquesne Weekend is widely remembered as a foundational moment in the modern Catholic Charismatic Renewal, which subsequently spread through prayer groups, communities and ministries around the world.

Major Catholic writings on the Holy Spirit

Divinum Illud Munus — Leo XIII

1897

Pope Leo XIII devoted this encyclical to the presence and action of the Holy Spirit. He links Baptism with regeneration, Confirmation with a fuller strengthening by the Spirit, calls the Spirit the supreme Gift, and urges Christians to know, love and invoke Him more earnestly.

Read Divinum Illud Munus

Dominum et Vivificantem — St. John Paul II

1986

This encyclical presents the Holy Spirit as Lord and giver of life, a Divine Person at the center of Christian faith and the source and dynamic power of the Church’s renewal. It explicitly recalls Leo XIII’s earlier teaching.

Read Dominum et Vivificantem

Catechism & Vatican II

The Catechism gathers Scripture, Tradition and conciliar teaching on the Spirit’s mission, His names and symbols, Pentecost, the Church and charisms. Vatican II repeatedly describes the Spirit as sanctifying, guiding and enriching the Church with hierarchical and charismatic gifts.

Intercession

The Catechism describes intercession as petition on behalf of another and participation in Christ’s own intercession. Christian intercession has no narrow boundary: we pray for the Church, rulers, the suffering, sinners, persecutors, enemies, the conversion of souls and the needs of the whole world. The Holy Spirit Himself helps our weakness and intercedes according to God’s will.

CHARIS has an international Intercession Commission, and CHARIS India has the National Intercession Network (NIN), whose stated aims include authentic formation, prayer for revival, conversion of souls and peace.

Prophetic Intercession

Prophetic intercession is a charismatic pastoral expression for intercession that is attentive to Scripture, the needs and “signs of the times,” and possible promptings or charisms of the Holy Spirit. CHARIS and CHARIS India both use the term in intercession formation.

It must never be confused with new public revelation or unquestionable prediction. A word, image, burden or impression received in prayer must be tested. It should conform to Sacred Scripture and Catholic faith, bear the fruits of the Spirit, respect human freedom, avoid fear and manipulation, and remain open to communal and pastoral discernment. Charisms exist to build up the Body of Christ, not to establish personal authority.

Veni Creator Spiritus

Latin

Veni, Creator Spiritus, mentes tuorum visita, imple superna gratia, quae tu creasti pectora. Qui diceris Paraclitus, altissimi donum Dei, fons vivus, ignis, caritas, et spiritalis unctio. Tu septiformis munere, digitus paternae dexterae, tu rite promissum Patris, sermone ditans guttura. Accende lumen sensibus, infunde amorem cordibus, infirma nostri corporis virtute firmans perpeti. Hostem repellas longius pacemque dones protinus; ductore sic te praevio vitemus omne noxium. Per te sciamus da Patrem, noscamus atque Filium, teque utriusque Spiritum credamus omni tempore. Deo Patri sit gloria, et Filio, qui a mortuis surrexit, ac Paraclito, in saeculorum saecula. Amen.

English

Come, Creator Spirit; visit the minds of Your faithful. Fill with heavenly grace the hearts that You created. You are called the Advocate, the gift of God Most High, the living fountain, fire, love, and spiritual anointing. You are sevenfold in Your gifts, the finger of the Father’s right hand, the promised One of the Father, enriching our speech with Your word. Kindle light in our senses; pour love into our hearts; strengthen the weakness of our bodies with enduring power. Drive the enemy far away and grant us peace without delay. With You going before us as our guide, may we avoid everything harmful. Through You grant that we may know the Father and also know the Son, and that we may always believe in You, the Spirit of them both. Glory be to God the Father, and to the Son who rose from the dead, and to the Advocate, for ages of ages. Amen.

Veni Sancte Spiritus

Latin

Veni, Sancte Spiritus, et emitte caelitus lucis tuae radium. Veni, pater pauperum, veni, dator munerum, veni, lumen cordium. Consolator optime, dulcis hospes animae, dulce refrigerium. In labore requies, in aestu temperies, in fletu solatium. O lux beatissima, reple cordis intima tuorum fidelium. Sine tuo numine, nihil est in homine, nihil est innoxium. Lava quod est sordidum, riga quod est aridum, sana quod est saucium. Flecte quod est rigidum, fove quod est frigidum, rege quod est devium. Da tuis fidelibus, in te confidentibus, sacrum septenarium. Da virtutis meritum, da salutis exitum, da perenne gaudium. Amen. Alleluia.

English

Come, Holy Spirit, and send from heaven a ray of Your light. Come, Father of the poor; come, giver of gifts; come, light of hearts. Best Consoler, sweet guest of the soul, sweet refreshment. In labor, rest; in heat, relief; in tears, consolation. O most blessed Light, fill the inmost heart of Your faithful. Without Your divine power there is nothing in man, nothing that is innocent. Wash what is unclean; water what is dry; heal what is wounded. Bend what is rigid; warm what is cold; guide what has gone astray. Give to Your faithful, who trust in You, Your sacred sevenfold gift. Give the reward of virtue; give the end of salvation; give everlasting joy. Amen. Alleluia.
