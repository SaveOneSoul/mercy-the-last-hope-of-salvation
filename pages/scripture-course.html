Catholic Biblical Studies

A 27-unit Scripture pathway covering canon, Old and New Testament backgrounds, culture, languages, textual criticism, authorial intention, original-language meaning, hermeneutics, exegesis, biblical dictionary work, commentaries and archaeology.

Professional-style, not accredited. The course is designed to resemble serious seminary/university study in breadth, source discipline and assignments. It does not claim ecclesiastical or academic accreditation.

How every passage should be studied

1. Text

Establish the text, translation, textual witnesses, grammar and lexical range.

2. Context

Identify genre, historical setting, culture, audience, literary structure and immediate argument.

3. Meaning

Ask what the human author intends to communicate and how the original hearers/readers would likely understand it.

4. Canon & Church

Read within the whole canon, Tradition, Fathers and Magisterium without bypassing the literal sense.

Major course blocks

Old Testament background

Ancient Near East, covenant, monarchy, Assyria, Babylon, exile, Persian restoration, Hellenistic culture, Second Temple developments, kinship, purity, temple, sacrifice, honor-shame and social institutions.

New Testament background

Second Temple Judaism, Roman rule, Herodian politics, synagogue and temple, Pharisees, Sadducees, scribes, apocalyptic hope, patronage, slavery, citizenship, household structures and Greco-Roman rhetoric.

Original languages

Biblical Hebrew, Aramaic and Koine Greek: morphology, syntax, semantics, discourse, textual witnesses, lemma versus inflected form, semantic range and translation decisions.

Hermeneutics & exegesis

Literal sense, spiritual senses, typology, historical-critical approaches, narrative, rhetorical, canonical and intertextual methods, plus Catholic interpretive principles from Dei Verbum.

Reference tools

Biblical dictionaries, lexicons, concordances, atlases, scholarly commentaries, patristic catenae and Magisterial documents—each used according to its authority and purpose.

Biblical archaeology

Inscriptions, coins, settlements, temples, roads, tombs, pottery, architecture and imperial records are classified as direct attestation, contextual evidence, disputed identification or reconstruction.

Background of all 73 Catholic biblical books

Each dossier includes testament, literary section, authorship/tradition, composition, audience, purpose, genre, original language, themes, cultural-historical context and study questions. Authorship and dating are presented with appropriate scholarly caution rather than as false certainty.

Recommended scholarly workflow

  1. Read the entire biblical book before isolating verses.
  2. Build the book dossier: structure, genre, setting, audience, purpose, themes and textual history.
  3. Translate or compare original-language clauses where relevant; do not reduce a word to one English gloss.
  4. Perform exegesis of the immediate literary unit before systematic theology.
  5. Compare at least two serious commentaries and identify where they agree or differ.
  6. Check archaeology and external history only for claims those disciplines can actually address.
  7. Read patristic and Magisterial interpretation after establishing the literal-historical sense.
  8. Write a synthesis distinguishing original sense, canonical development, doctrinal reception and contemporary application.
