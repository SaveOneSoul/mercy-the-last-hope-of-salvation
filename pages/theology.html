Theological principle. Catholic theology serves the faith received by the Church. It seeks deeper understanding without treating personal speculation as doctrine. Where questions admit legitimate theological diversity, this page distinguishes that diversity from truths taught definitively or authoritatively by the Church.
How to study

Faith, reason and the sources of theology

Theology is rational reflection on divine Revelation received in faith. Catholic theology therefore listens to Scripture in the living Tradition of the Church, attends to the Magisterium, learns from the Fathers and Doctors, and uses philosophy, history and the human sciences according to their proper competence.

1. Receive Revelation

God reveals Himself in salvation history, culminating in Jesus Christ. Sacred Scripture and Sacred Tradition transmit the one deposit of faith entrusted to the Church.

2. Read with the Church

The Magisterium authentically interprets the Word of God. Theology is not a private authority above Scripture, Tradition or the teaching office of the Church.

3. Seek understanding

Faith and reason are ordered toward truth. Theology asks how doctrines cohere, what they mean, how they developed in expression and how they illuminate Christian life and mission.

Study pattern

One method across every discipline

Each field on this page is organized through the same six lenses:

Church Teaching → Scripture → Fathers & Doctors → Councils & Magisterium → Major Questions → Further Study

Levels of certainty matter

Dogma, definitive teaching, authoritative non-definitive teaching, common theological conclusions and permitted opinions should not be presented as if they all carried the same doctrinal weight.

Discipline 1

Foundations of Theology

Before studying individual doctrines, theology asks how God reveals, how faith knows, and how the Church receives and interprets Revelation.

Church Teaching

One deposit of faith

Sacred Scripture and Sacred Tradition are closely bound together and communicate the Word of God. The Magisterium serves that Word and does not stand above it.

Scripture

God speaks in history

Luke 1:1–4; John 1:1–18; 2 Timothy 3:14–17; 2 Thessalonians 2:15; Hebrews 1:1–2 provide important biblical entry points.

Faith handed on

Councils & Magisterium

Vatican I’s Dei Filius, Vatican II’s Dei Verbum, the Catechism and St. John Paul II’s Fides et Ratio are foundational.

Discipline 2

Trinitarian Theology

The Trinity is the central mystery of Christian faith and life: one God in three distinct divine Persons—Father, Son and Holy Spirit.

Church Teaching

One nature, three Persons

The Persons are really distinct from one another yet each is wholly the one God. The divine unity is not divided among Father, Son and Spirit.

Scripture

Revealed through Christ

Matthew 28:19; John 1:1–18; John 14–17; 2 Corinthians 13:13 and Ephesians 1:3–14 are major Trinitarian texts.

Study Matthew 28:19 in Logos →
Fathers & Doctors

Nicaea to Augustine

Sts. Athanasius, Basil the Great, Gregory of Nazianzus, Gregory of Nyssa and Augustine were decisive in articulating orthodox Trinitarian faith.

Councils & Magisterium

Nicaea I, Constantinople I and the later conciliar tradition established the Church’s normative language concerning the divinity of the Son and Holy Spirit.

Catechism on the Trinity

Major questions

How can God be one and three? What are the eternal processions? What are the divine missions? How does Trinitarian theology shape prayer, grace, Church and mission?

Discipline 3

Christology & Soteriology

Christology studies who Jesus Christ is; soteriology studies the saving work accomplished in Him.

Church Teaching

True God and true man

The eternal Son assumed a complete human nature without ceasing to be divine. Jesus Christ is one divine Person in two natures, without confusion, change, division or separation.

Scripture

Incarnation and Paschal mystery

John 1:1–18; Philippians 2:5–11; Colossians 1:15–20; Hebrews 1–2; the Passion narratives and 1 Corinthians 15 are central.

Study John 1:1 in Logos →
Fathers & Doctors

From Athanasius to Aquinas

St. Athanasius, St. Cyril of Alexandria, St. Leo the Great, St. Anselm and St. Thomas Aquinas provide major lines of reflection on Incarnation and redemption.

Councils & Magisterium

Ephesus and Chalcedon are indispensable for Christology. The Catechism integrates Incarnation, Cross, descent among the dead, Resurrection and Ascension.

Catechism — Jesus Christ

Major questions

How do Christ’s divine and human natures relate? What does it mean that the Cross is sacrifice, satisfaction, redemption and victory? How are grace, freedom and human cooperation related to salvation?

Pneumatology — Theology of the Holy Spirit

Pneumatology studies the divine Person and mission of the Holy Spirit in Christ, the Church, sanctification, charisms and mission.

Church Teaching

Lord and giver of life

The Holy Spirit is true God, consubstantial with the Father and the Son. He sanctifies, gives life, builds the Church and distributes gifts for the common good.

Scripture

John 14–16; Acts 1–2; Romans 8; 1 Corinthians 12–14 and Galatians 5 are major texts.

Councils & Magisterium

Discipline 5

Ecclesiology — Theology of the Church

Ecclesiology studies the Church as mystery, sacrament of salvation, People of God, Body of Christ and Temple of the Holy Spirit.

Church Teaching

One, holy, catholic and apostolic

The Church is founded by Christ, animated by the Holy Spirit and ordered through communion of faith, sacraments and pastoral governance.

Scripture

People, Body and Bride

Matthew 16:13–20; Acts 2:42–47; 1 Corinthians 12; Ephesians 2 and 5; 1 Peter 2:4–10.

Fathers & Doctors

Communion through history

Sts. Ignatius of Antioch, Cyprian, Augustine and later ecclesiological traditions illuminate episcopacy, Eucharistic communion and the Church’s unity.

Councils & Magisterium

Vatican I and Vatican II, especially Lumen Gentium and Unitatis Redintegratio, are essential.

Discipline 6

Mariology — Mary in the Mystery of Christ and the Church

Catholic Mariology is Christological and ecclesial: every authentic Marian doctrine safeguards truths about Christ, grace, redemption and the destiny of the Church.

Church Teaching

Four Marian dogmas

Mary is Mother of God, perpetually virgin, immaculately conceived and assumed body and soul into heavenly glory.

Scripture

Daughter of Zion and Mother of the Lord

Genesis 3:15; Luke 1–2; John 2:1–11; John 19:25–27; Acts 1:14 and Revelation 12 are major texts in the Catholic tradition.

Study Luke 1:28 in Logos →
Fathers & Doctors

New Eve and Theotokos

St. Irenaeus’ New Eve typology and the patristic defense of Mary as Theotokos are foundational for later Mariology.

Councils & Magisterium

Ephesus, Ineffabilis Deus, Munificentissimus Deus and Vatican II’s Lumen Gentium chapter VIII are key.

Major questions

How does Marian devotion differ from worship due to God alone? How do Marian doctrines illuminate grace and salvation? What belongs to doctrine, approved devotion or theological proposal?

Discipline 7

Church Teaching

Seven sacraments

Scripture

Visible signs, saving action

Mystagogy and sacramental realism

Councils & Magisterium

Trent and Vatican II are indispensable. Sacrosanctum Concilium articulates the nature of the liturgy and active participation.

Major questions

Discipline 8

Moral Theology

Moral theology studies human acts in the light of the call to beatitude, grace, freedom, virtue, conscience, law and communion with God.

Church Teaching

Life in Christ

The moral life is more than rule-keeping: it is conversion and growth in charity through grace, virtues, the Beatitudes and the commandments.

Scripture

The way of discipleship

Exodus 20; Deuteronomy 6; Matthew 5–7; Matthew 22:34–40; Romans 12–14; Galatians 5 and James are foundational.

Virtue, grace and charity

Augustine and Aquinas are central for grace, law, virtue, conscience and the ordering of human life toward God.

Councils & Magisterium

The Catechism Part Three, Veritatis Splendor, Catholic social doctrine and contemporary magisterial teaching on human dignity are key.

Discipline 9

Spiritual & Mystical Theology

Spiritual theology studies growth in holiness and communion with God: prayer, asceticism, virtue, purification, illumination, union and discernment.

Church Teaching

Universal call to holiness

Every baptized person is called to holiness. Mystical grace is never separated from faith, charity, sacraments, moral conversion and ecclesial communion.

Scripture

Abide in Christ

Psalms; Matthew 6; John 15–17; Romans 8; 2 Corinthians 3; Philippians 3 and 1 Thessalonians 5 provide essential foundations.

Study John 15:5 in Logos →
Fathers & Doctors

Schools of holiness

The Desert Fathers, St. Augustine, St. Bernard, St. Teresa of Ávila, St. John of the Cross, St. Thérèse and St. Ignatius offer distinct but complementary schools.

Magisterium & tradition

Vatican II’s universal call to holiness, the Catechism’s teaching on prayer and the witness of canonized saints provide a secure framework.

Major questions

How do ascetical effort and grace relate? What is contemplation? How should extraordinary experiences be discerned? What are purification, illumination and union? How do spiritualities differ while remaining Catholic?

Discipline 10

Eschatology — The Last Things

Eschatology studies the fulfillment of God’s saving plan in the individual person and in creation: death, judgment, heaven, purgatory, hell, resurrection and the final coming of Christ.

Church Teaching

Christian hope

Death ends earthly pilgrimage. Each person faces particular judgment; history moves toward Christ’s return, the resurrection of the dead and final judgment.

Scripture

Resurrection and new creation

Daniel 12; Matthew 25; John 5:19–29; 1 Corinthians 15; 1 Thessalonians 4–5 and Revelation 20–22 are major texts.

Study 1 Corinthians 15:20 in Logos →
Fathers & Doctors

Hope purified of speculation

Patristic and scholastic theology reflects on resurrection, judgment, beatific vision and purification while warning against idle prediction of times and dates.

Councils & Magisterium

The creeds, medieval councils, Benedict XII’s Benedictus Deus, Vatican II and the Catechism form the principal doctrinal framework.

Major questions

What is the beatific vision? What is purgatory? How should biblical apocalyptic language be interpreted? What is the relation between particular and final judgment? What does “new heavens and a new earth” mean?
